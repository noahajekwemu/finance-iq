# Finance IQ Model #001 — 13-Week Cash Flow Forecast

**Finance IQ with Noah Ajekwemu**

> A practical short-term cash-flow forecasting model for management liquidity planning and decision support.

## Overview

Finance IQ Model #001 — 13-Week Cash Flow Forecast is the first flagship release in the Finance IQ Model Series.

The model addresses a fundamental corporate-finance problem:

**A business can be profitable and still run out of cash.**

The model provides a forward-looking view of expected cash inflows, cash outflows and closing cash balances over a 13-week planning horizon.

It is designed to help management move from retrospective financial reporting towards proactive liquidity management.

## Business Problem

Traditional monthly financial reporting can leave management with insufficient visibility over near-term liquidity.

A business may report revenue growth or accounting profit while simultaneously experiencing:

- Working-capital pressure
- Delayed customer collections
- Large supplier payments
- Rising operating costs
- Timing mismatches between inflows and outflows
- Declining available cash

The 13-week forecast focuses on the timing of cash rather than accounting profit alone.

## What the Model Supports

The model is intended to help management:

- Forecast weekly cash movements.
- Identify periods of liquidity pressure.
- Monitor projected closing cash.
- Understand the timing of inflows and outflows.
- Test alternative scenarios.
- Support short-term cash-management decisions.
- Provide an executive view of the business's liquidity position.

## Core Architecture

The workbook follows a structured financial-modelling approach built around:

**Inputs → Calculations → Scenarios → Dashboard → Decision Support**

The model includes the core components required for a professional forecasting tool, including assumptions, inputs, calculation logic, scenario analysis, dashboard reporting and executive interpretation.

## Scenario Analysis

Scenario analysis allows management to assess how changes in operating assumptions can affect the projected cash position.

The objective is not to predict one certain outcome. It is to understand the range of plausible liquidity outcomes and identify where management attention may be required.

## Executive Perspective

The central management question is not simply:

> "Will the company make a profit?"

It is:

> "Will the company have enough cash, when it needs it?"

A 13-week cash-flow forecast helps answer that question by putting cash timing at the centre of short-term financial decision-making.

## Why 13 Weeks?

A 13-week horizon provides a practical bridge between daily cash management and longer-term financial planning.

It is long enough to expose upcoming liquidity pressures while remaining short enough for management to work with relatively concrete operational assumptions.
